Russian Holidays.
History, Dates & Precision.

A focused interactive lesson for an upper-intermediate / advanced student preparing for the Russian Unified State Exam in English (EGE 2027).

B2–C1 90–100 minutes Listening · Spelling · Writing

Teacher methodology

  1. Diagnose first: do not pre-teach all answers. Record what breaks down: sound recognition, factual understanding, inference, or spelling.
  2. Two-pass listening: first listen for meaning; second listen for evidence and distractors.
  3. Evidence-based feedback: ask the learner to cite the exact sentence that proves each answer.
  4. Reconstruction: dictate dates/phrases immediately after listening and again at the next lesson.
  5. Transfer: use the same content in EGE task 37 with a strict writing checklist.

Warm-up discussion

Which national holiday is the most closely connected to Russian history? Why? Can a holiday have both a religious and a secular meaning? Why might a foreign visitor misunderstand the date of Orthodox Christmas?

Dates & cultural literacy

Say each date aloud using the first of January / January the first. Compare fixed and moveable celebrations.

Check your historical knowledge

Teacher note: avoid date traps

Orthodox Christmas: 7 January, with Christmas Eve on 6 January. Old New Year: night of 13–14 January. Maslenitsa: changes each year; 8–14 March in 2027. 23 February: Defender of the Fatherland Day. 1 May: Spring and Labour Day. 9 May: Victory Day. 12 June: Russia Day (not Russia's Independence Day). 4 November: National Unity Day.

Date prompts based on the student's topic list; holiday schedules should always be checked for the specific year.

Exam strategy

In False, the speaker contradicts the statement. In Not Stated, the conversation simply does not provide enough information. A plausible historical fact is not proof that the speaker said it.

Precision listening & spelling

Listen to one short phrase and type exactly what you hear. Pay attention to dates, historical vocabulary, and confusing word forms. Capitalisation and terminal punctuation are ignored, but spelling matters.

Teacher-only extension: stronger historical writing

After completing Task 37, give the student an optional 70-word challenge: “Explain why dates can carry different historical meanings for different generations.” Focus on accuracy rather than advanced vocabulary for its own sake.

Hi Alex,

Thanks for your email. Your history project sounds really interesting!

Victory Day, celebrated on 9 May, commemorates the Soviet Union's victory over Nazi Germany in 1945. Many people attend commemorative ceremonies, lay flowers at memorials and remember relatives who experienced the war. I'd also recommend learning about National Unity Day on 4 November because it highlights an important turning point in Russian history.

It's great that you've started volunteering at a history museum! What do you usually do there? How many hours a week do you spend volunteering? Do you work with visitors?

Anyway, I hope your project goes well. Write back soon!

Best wishes,
Sam

Review, retrieve, repeat

Exit ticket

Explain, without notes, the difference between the fourth of November and the fourteenth of January. Then describe one historical holiday in 30 seconds.

Error classification

For each mistake label the cause: S = sound recognition; M = misunderstanding; D = distractor; O = orthography; T = task requirements.

Spacing

Repeat five missed dates or terms after 48 hours, one week, and two weeks. Reuse them in new sentences rather than simply rereading answers.

Independent work (25–40 minutes)

  1. Complete the listening tasks from the selected Решу ЕГЭ variant №3545193.
  2. Write down the evidence for each incorrect answer and re-listen with the transcript if available.
  3. Complete 2027 demo task 37 (Victory Day) under timed conditions.
  4. Submit a short error log: 3 mistakes, 3 explanations, 3 corrected examples.

Suggested longer-term programme

Every week: two short focused listening drills, one writing task, three 5-minute retrieval sessions on spelling and numbers. Every 2–3 weeks: one timed section plus detailed error analysis. Every 4–6 weeks: a full diagnostic paper; adjust the plan using error rates by task type rather than total score alone.

Exam-task differentiation: this lesson contains a Task 2–style listening and Task 37–style email; it does not replace practice in Tasks 1 and 3–9, Task 38, grammar, or speaking.
