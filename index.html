Reading 1 · Andrew: When Gaming Took Over

A1–A2 short interview · about 200 words · based only on the interview provided

Andrew started playing video games at a neighbour's house when he was about nine or ten. When he was around twelve or thirteen, he got an Xbox. His mother tried to limit his gaming to one or two hours a day. At first, Andrew played sports games. Later, he played games such as Call of Duty and Halo.

In his junior year of high school, Andrew moved to California and lost many of his friends. Gaming helped him stay in contact with old friends and also gave him an escape.

In his second semester of college, gaming became a serious problem. Andrew skipped classes and homework and played games all day. He failed all his classes and was dismissed from college. Later, he went to community college, but the same problem happened again. His family tried to talk to him about his gaming several times.

At the age of twenty-two, Andrew stopped gaming completely. About a year later, he felt closer to his family and friends and became more productive. He also found other hobbies, including photography and camping.

Andrew says that many people can manage gaming without a problem, but some people are at risk. He thinks young people should have other hobbies too, and he suggests keeping a game console in a public part of the home instead of simply banning games.

Can & Should · Benefits, Problems & Advice

Use can / can't for ability or possible results, and should / shouldn't for advice.

can / can't + base verb → Games can develop problem-solving skills.
should / shouldn't + base verb → Players should take a break.
After these modal verbs, use the base verb: can help, not can helps; should take, not should to take.

Code Names · Teacher + Student

You play together against a virtual Opponent. Use the same Game Code on both computers.

How to play / Как играть
Very simple rules / Очень простые правила
Teacher = Spymaster I know the secret. Я знаю секрет.
Student = Guesser I guess the words. Я угадываю слова.
Virtual Opponent Computer player. Виртуальный соперник.

🇬🇧 English

1You play together. Teacher knows the secret. Student guesses.
2Teacher opens the key. Only Teacher sees which words are ours, Opponent, Neutral and Danger.
3Teacher gives one clue + a number. Example: school — 2.
4Student guesses. After every guess, both players click the same word on their own screens.
5Both press Pass turn. Then the Virtual Opponent automatically takes one Opponent word.
6Win: find all Our team words first. Lose: click Danger or let the Opponent finish first.

3Учитель говорит одно слово + число. Например: school — 2.
4Ученик угадывает. После каждой догадки оба нажимают одно и то же слово на своих экранах.
5Оба нажимают Pass turn. Потом виртуальный соперник автоматически забирает одно своё слово.
