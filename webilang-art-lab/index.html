01True story · illustrated comic + mediation mission

The Painting They
Destroyed

New York, 1933. Diego Rivera is painting Man at the Crossroads for Rockefeller Center. His huge mural contrasts two possible futures: the world of wealthy elites, conflict, and industrial power with one of workers, science, and collective action. Rivera included Lenin as a symbol of international workers’ solidarity and his communist political beliefs. Nelson Rockefeller objected to this recognizable political leader appearing in a mural commissioned by his family. What happens when the artist and patron cannot agree? Read the story and decide what YOU would do. (Dialogue is dramatized, not a verbatim transcript.)

What was Rivera trying to say?

Man at the Crossroads explores a choice between different futures for humanity. It places science, industry, and people at the centre, contrasting social divisions, wealth and conflict with collective action and possibilities for a more equal world. Why Lenin? Rivera was a communist; depicting Lenin holding hands with workers made his view of international solidarity visible. Rockefeller regarded this as inappropriate for his family’s commissioned building. The mural was never simply about one face—it was about who gets to decide what art can say.

03Reading · reality vs value

The $6 Million Banana

🍌
MAURIZIO CATTELAN · COMEDIAN

Conceptual illustration; not a photograph of the artwork.

Imagine entering an art gallery. On a white wall, you see an ordinary banana attached with grey tape. Is this a joke? Or is it a serious artwork?

Italian artist Maurizio Cattelan first presented Comedian in 2019. The banana was real, and it could be replaced when it became old. The important part was not one particular banana: it was the artist's idea and the instructions for showing the artwork.

In November 2024, an edition of Comedian sold at Sotheby's in New York for $6.2 million, including fees. Some people thought the price was ridiculous. Others said it was a clever question about money, art, and what people consider valuable.

So what did the collector buy? Not a special fruit that lasts forever, but an artwork supported by a certificate and display instructions. Perhaps the artwork is also the conversation it creates.
