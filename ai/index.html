03 / Historical inquiry · Eugenics and bias

Eugenics, measurement and algorithmic bias

The film's earlier historical chapter links attempts to measure human intelligence with the history of eugenics. It discusses Francis Galton, Karl Pearson, intelligence testing and harmful uses of quantitative classification. This tab extends that discussion; it is not a claim that all of this material appears in the first ten minutes or the 50–60-minute excerpt.

Working definition: Eugenics was a movement advocating the deliberate control of human reproduction to promote supposedly desirable inherited traits. Its history includes racist classifications, coercion and forced sterilisation.

Algorithmic bias means systematic patterns in the development or outcomes of computational systems that create unfair or unequal effects, for example because of unrepresentative data, inappropriate proxies, design choices or deployment context.

Case study: An AI placement test

An online English test predicts that students who speak with one particular accent have lower proficiency, even when independent teachers rate them equally well.

  1. Identify the possible source of the bias.
  2. Explain how you would test the system for unequal error rates.
  3. Propose a response that preserves learner dignity and teacher judgment.
Possible cause: uneven representation of accents in training or evaluation data. Audit: compare results across accent groups while controlling for independently assessed proficiency, with informed consent and careful interpretation. Remedy: human review, transparent scoring rubrics, broader validation data and a right to contest results.

Discussion

Distinguish historical continuity from historical determinism. Does a problematic intellectual genealogy settle the ethics of present-day AI?

05 / Speaking · C1 · Music, authorship and artistic agency

Nick Cave: Did AI change his mind?

Study two sources together: The Independent article (11 November 2026) and the AI-animated Tupelo video (2025), directed by Andrew Dominik. Use the evidence to explain exactly what changed in Cave's position—and what did not.

Nick Cave · Wikimedia Commons

Nick Cave is an Australian singer-songwriter, author and frontman of Nick Cave & the Bad Seeds, known for dark narrative songs that combine blues, post-punk, biblical imagery and questions of grief, faith and creativity.

The nuance: Cave criticised the replacement of songwriting by generative AI. In July 2025 he praised a human filmmaker's use of AI to animate Elvis photographs for the Tupelo video. In November 2026 he again strongly criticised AI song generators. His view became more differentiated, not unconditionally favourable to AI.

Step 3 · Trace a change of mind

2023 · Rejection

Cave objects to AI imitating human songwriting and displacing lived experience.

Discussion · C1

  1. What was Cave's initial objection to AI-generated songs? Was he challenging the technology's technical quality, or its understanding of human experience?
  2. What precisely did Dominik do with AI in the Tupelo film? Why is this different from prompting a system to compose an entirely new song?
  3. Which qualities of the video might have changed Cave's mind: its imagery, mythic references, emotional effect, or Dominik's artistic direction?
  4. Does Cave's 2026 criticism contradict his 2025 response, or can both be consistent with the same principles about authorship?
  5. Where should we draw the line between AI augmenting an artist's vision and AI substituting for an artist? Apply your criterion to photography, cinema and music.
  6. Can a work produced partly through generative tools communicate genuine human emotion? Does its value depend on the creator, the process, or the audience's experience?
  7. Why might the ability to revise one's opinions be a sign of intellectual strength rather than inconsistency? What evidence would persuade you to change your own mind?
  8. Is the most useful question “Was AI used?” or “Who made the consequential creative decisions?” Defend your answer with examples.

Nick Cave · Artist profile

Nick Cave (born 1957) is an Australian singer, songwriter, novelist and musician best known as the frontman of Nick Cave & the Bad Seeds. His work spans post-punk, blues, alternative rock and intensely narrative ballads.

His music often explores mortality, loss, redemption, religious imagery and the relationship between art and lived experience. He writes about creativity and culture in The Red Hand Files, a website where he responds to readers' questions.

For this lesson, notice that Cave distinguishes between an artist using AI to realise an idea and a system generating a song while removing the artist from the process.

Portrait: Wikimedia Commons · photograph and licensing details.

Reference note · What does “Tupelo” mean?

The setting: Tupelo, Mississippi, is Elvis Presley's birthplace. In Nick Cave & the Bad Seeds' 1985 song, the birth of Elvis is reimagined as a dark, almost biblical event amid a threatening storm. The album title, The Firstborn Is Dead, also evokes the death of Elvis's twin brother at birth.

The sound and imagery: An insistent bass line, dramatic incantation and images of storms and floods transform a biographical event into a story of catastrophe and salvation. The song draws partly on John Lee Hooker's blues narrative about a Tupelo flood.

The 2025 video: Filmmaker Andrew Dominik used AI to animate historical still images of Elvis. Cave praised the result as an imaginative interpretation—he did not say that AI wrote the song or created its emotional meaning on its own.

Interpretive question: If the film evokes resurrection by animating a photograph of someone who has died, is the technological effect appropriate to the song's themes—or does it introduce new ethical concerns?

This is an interpretive teaching note, not a definitive explanation. Read Cave's account.
